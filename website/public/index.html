The Nereus way

Built around how you operate.

One station engine. Native consoles on the devices you use. A workspace you can make your own, with the radio's capabilities and the people sharing it built into the way it works.

01 / THE SIGNAL

Live radio data. Drawn locally.

Your device renders calibrated spectrum and waterfall data from the Core. Clarity follows the band noise floor to keep the waterfall readable as conditions change.

02 / YOUR WORKSPACE

Arrange the station your way.

Build a desktop workspace from pans, receiver slices and meters. Each connected device has its own view and operating state, with markers showing where others are listening.

03 / SHARED CONTROL

A real radio, shared thoughtfully.

Pair with your Core, work within the radio's available receivers, and see who holds transmit. Confirm receiver changes that affect another device before they happen.

NereusSDR for iPhone & iPad

The whole station. In your hand.

A native app built for touch. See the band, tune a signal, hear your station and transmit from your phone. Your Core runs the radio and DSP; the app brings its controls to you.

01 / TUNE

Find your signal.

Spectrum and waterfall drawn in Metal. Drag the band, turn a dial or enter a precise frequency. Keep your receiver flags and spots in view.

02 / LISTEN

Hear your station.

Receive audio on your speaker or headphones. Set modes, filters, AGC and noise reduction on the Core. Keep listening with the screen locked.

03 / TRANSMIT

Take the microphone.

Voice transmit with on-screen PTT. The Core manages transmit ownership, time-outs and interlocks, with one device holding transmit at a time.

Screenshots from the native iOS simulator with simulated station data. Controls available depend on your radio and Core.

NereusCore · At the station

A small computer. A complete station engine.

Run the headless Core beside the radio, or use it with the desktop console on the same computer. The Core owns the radio connection, receive and transmit DSP, spectrum and station state. Your clients receive audio and display frames and send controls back.

  1. 01 / RADIO

    Your OpenHPSDR radio

    RF and I/Q over Ethernet.
    Protocol 1 or Protocol 2.

  2. 02 / CORE

    NereusCore

    Radio, DSP and station controls.
    On a desktop or a Linux SBC.

  3. 03 / CONSOLE

    Your choice of screen

    macOS, Windows or Linux.
    Native iPhone and iPad.

Single-board computers

Keep the processing beside the radio.

Development and hardware testing have used a Raspberry Pi 4 with a Hermes Lite 2 and a Radxa ROCK 5C with an ANAN-G2/Saturn. Receiver count and DSP choices depend on the radio and the computer's capacity.

Linux ARM64 Core packages and station-image build recipes provide the starting point for a dedicated station computer.

Local and remote operation

Connect from home or away.

Find and pair with your Core on the LAN, or connect from another network. The remote access service introduces the two ends, using direct connections where possible and relay paths when needed.

Audio, display frames and controls travel over an encrypted link. The high-rate radio I/Q stays at the station.

Remote access and self-hosting →

One station, several devices

Up to four devices share a Core.

Desktop and mobile clients use the same station, each with its own pans and slices. Receiver access is coordinated, other devices' slices are visible as markers, and one device holds transmit at a time.

The radio's available receivers and the Core's processing capacity set what the station can run together.

The desktop console

A familiar console. A new station behind it.

Run locally or connect to your Core. Flexible panadapters, receiver slices, meters and station tools on macOS, Windows and Linux.

A simulated 40 m band, drawn live in your browser in the program's own layout. Click or tap the waterfall to tune.

Features

The whole station, in one console.

Follow a band, pull a voice out of the noise, shape your transmitted audio, or work a digital mode. The controls follow your radio and Core's capabilities, with clear reasons when a tool is unavailable.

RX DSP

WDSP does the heavy lifting.

WDSP handles SSB, CW, AM, FM and digital modes. Choose conventional, spectral or neural noise reduction, including the new WDSP NNR. Available algorithms and models follow your Core's capabilities; NNR can step back its model under processing load.

  • AGC
  • NB
  • NB2
  • SNB
  • NR2
  • NR3
  • NR4
  • DFNR
  • NNR
  • MNR
  • ANF
  • APF
  • SQL
  • RIT

TX

Voice transmit with a full speech chain.

SSB, AM, SAM and DSB transmit, with a 10-band EQ, leveler, ALC, CFC compressor, CESSB and phase rotator. PureSignal predistortion runs on every supported radio, the Hermes Lite 2 included.

  • EQ
  • LEV
  • ALC
  • CFC
  • CPDR
  • CESSB
  • VOX
  • DEXP
  • PS-A
  • 2-Tone

Panadapter

A panadapter drawn on your GPU.

Arrange multiple pans and receiver slices, float a pan onto another monitor, and tune with CTUN and zoom. Explore signals over time in the GPU-rendered 3D spectrum, with Clarity following the noise floor to keep the waterfall readable.

  • Metal
  • Vulkan
  • D3D12
  • CTUN
  • Zoom
  • Clarity
  • Multiple pans
  • 3D spectrum

Meters

Meters you can build yourself.

Vintage analog S-meter faces, the ANAN multimeter, and cross-needle power and SWR meters. The AM modulation monitor adds positive and negative peaks, asymmetry and an envelope scope. Arrange instruments in docked or floating containers.

  • S-meter
  • Multimeter
  • Cross-needle
  • Vintage faces
  • AM monitor
  • Containers

Interference

Cut a carrier where it lives.

Place a tunable notch on the panadapter, then move, resize or bypass it. Notches stay at their RF frequency across retuning and apply across live receivers. Visual notch indicators leave noise-floor and meter calculations intact.

  • TNF
  • RF anchored
  • Persistent
  • Move / resize
  • Bypass

Receiver audio

A listening place for every slice.

Give each receiver its own gain, mute and listening position. Route slices to speakers or headphones, keeping a conversation in one ear or a second listening task on its own output.

  • Per-slice gain
  • Mute
  • Pan
  • Binaural
  • Speakers / headphones

Station tools

Plays well with your other software.

Spot Hub brings activity from DX clusters, RBN, POTA and reporters onto your panadapter for click-to-tune. Connect digital programs through TCI and virtual audio routing, and control Power Genius XL, Tuner Genius XL and RF-Kit RF2K-S from the console.

  • TCI
  • VAX audio
  • DX cluster
  • RBN
  • POTA
  • PSK Reporter
  • FreeDV Reporter

Diagnostics

See what the station is doing.

A consolidated radio-status dashboard brings measurements and connection health together. Inspect live connection counters, validate settings against your radio, export configuration and read logs in one place.

  • Radio status
  • Connection quality
  • Settings validation
  • Export / import
  • Logs

RADE

FreeDV RADE digital voice, built in.

RADE, FreeDV's machine-learning voice codec, runs as a native mode for receive and transmit with nothing else to install. The VFO shows SNR and, when it is sent, the other station's callsign.

Build from source

C++20 and Qt 6, built with CMake and Ninja. Install the dependencies for your platform from the build instructions, then: 

git clone https://github.com/boydsoftprez/NereusSDR.git
cd NereusSDR
cmake -B build -G Ninja -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=RelWithDebInfo
cmake --build build
./build/NereusSDR

Or take the signed source tarball from the release: NereusSDR-0.5.2-source.tar.gz

Heritage

Deep roots. Our own direction.

NereusSDR began by bringing Thetis's radio knowledge to native macOS, Windows and Linux consoles. It now adds its own station engine, remote media transport, shared-device operating model and native mobile app. Thetis, WDSP and the OpenHPSDR community remain part of its foundation, with upstream attribution preserved.

  1. FlexRadio Systems PowerSDR

    The Windows SDR console that Thetis descends from.

  2. OpenHPSDR community Thetis

    The standard console for ANAN and OpenHPSDR radios, carried forward by Doug Wigley W5WC, Richard Samphire MW0LGE and many other contributors.

  3. Native consoles · Shared station Core NereusSDR

    OpenHPSDR radio knowledge, a native desktop workspace, and original Core and mobile architecture for operating one station across devices.

WDSP

Warren Pratt NR0V's DSP library, the signal engine under both Thetis and NereusSDR.

AetherSDR

A native C++20 and Qt 6 FlexRadio client, and the architectural template for NereusSDR.

OpenHPSDR

The open hardware project whose Protocol 1 and Protocol 2 every supported radio speaks.
