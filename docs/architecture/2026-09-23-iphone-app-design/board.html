NereusSDR iPhone Layouts

NereusSDR on iPhone and iPad

The whole app

New this round, and kept: the notices and the states in between for several devices, first below. Then several devices on one Core at once, from your calls. Before them, the transmit time-out and your mic level on the band while keyed, then long sessions. Everything after that is agreed with your calls applied: a hardware PTT button that keys a locked phone through Apple's Push to Talk, audio and data use with cellular at Balanced, when things aren't right with the app keeping the older link too, FreeDV Reporter where a tap tunes, Spot Hub as one page, the iPad held upright with the applets below the band, spots on the band that never start behind a flag, the station's side with a switch in the desktop app, first launch with a station required, the tuning dial you pick in Setup, the same layout turned sideways, a lock screen that never transmits from its screen, and before them your layout with NereusSDR's flags and one flag rule for both devices, the tap-to-toggle PTT, the desktop's Setup tree, the front end in the Modes tab, and the amp and tuner in the TX panel.

Signals, station names, addresses and readings are examples. Each screen is its own phone; the tab bars don't switch screens. The iOS parts around NereusSDR's card (clock, status bar, the app behind the island) are drawn generic.

Several devices: states and notices

What the screens in the next section don't show: the messages that appear over the band when something happens involving another device, and the states in between (a device that's away, transmit on its way, a slice held by the radio's mic). The same cast as the next section: this phone has slice A on 40 m, the MacBook has slices B and D and has transmit, the iPad has slice C on 20 m.

Notices

Back, without transmit

The phone’s link dropped and it came back within 3 minutes. It didn’t have transmit, so there’s nothing about transmit to say.

Back, transmit taken meanwhile

It had transmit, and the MacBook took it while the phone was away. The phone doesn’t get it back by itself; Take it back asks first.

Back after 3 minutes

Away longer than 3 minutes: the Core saved its slices and brings them back, but transmit went free, so PTT reads Tap for whoever presses first.

Back after 3 minutes, Core full

Away longer than 3 minutes while four other devices filled the Core: the phone gets the fifth-device question, saying why.

A slice that couldn’t come back

Coming back, one of the phone’s slices finds every receiver taken. Its frequency and settings wait on the Core.

The antenna stays

Your call: tuning across a band edge doesn’t switch the antenna while another device listens through it, and the phone says so.

The Core is busy

The same chip as when the connection is full, when it’s the Core’s processing that runs short instead.

Short, nobody has transmit

With nobody holding transmit (or the radio’s mic holding it), no device keeps a full share, so the note says they share.

The states in between

A device that's away

The MacBook's link dropped while it had transmit. The Core keeps transmit for it, unkeyed, for up to 3 minutes. Its label greys with "away", and taking transmit asks without the red button, since nobody is on the air.

Transmit on its way

Right after Unkey and take over, the Core unkeys the MacBook, then hands transmit over. For that moment PTT waits with a turning ring; once it reads Tap, one tap keys.

Held by the radio's mic

On a Core with no desktop, the radio's own mic keys on the transmit slice, here this phone's slice A. The flag says ON AIR, the frequency greys, and tuning slice A waits until the press ends.

Away, on Devices

Setup, Devices marks a device whose link dropped as away, with how long. It keeps its place and its slices for 3 minutes.

Away, for a fifth device

A device that's away has been idle longest, so a fifth device's list starts on it.

Several devices at once

Your calls: up to four devices on one Core at the same time, each with its own slices and pans, and one of them holding transmit. Other devices’ slices show on your band, labelled and out of reach. Anything that would disturb another device asks first and names it, and that device is told, with Take it back. This replaces the takeover question: a second device now simply connects, and taking a place only comes up for a fifth. On every screen here this phone has slice A on 40 m, the MacBook at the shack has slices B (sharing this phone’s receiver) and D and has transmit, and the iPad has slice C on 20 m.

Slices and transmit

Another device’s slice

The MacBook’s slice B on your band: a dashed line in B’s colour and a label with the device’s name, but no flag, so there’s nothing to drag. The label opens a note saying whose it is. TX on the label and the MacBook on your PTT button both say it has transmit.

Taking transmit

Tap PTT while another device has transmit, and it asks first. Whoever has transmit is the control operator. Take transmit, then tap PTT again to go on the air.

While it’s on the air

Your call: the band shows the MacBook’s transmission, PTT turns red with its name, and the button says Unkey and take over. The Core unkeys the MacBook before transmit moves.

Transmit taken from you

The device that had transmit is told who took it and when. Take it back asks the same question the other way.

Taken at the radio

Your call: pressing the radio’s own PTT, its mic or a footswitch, takes transmit with no question, because the press is the answer. Programs never take transmit this way; they transmit only while their own device has it. PTT names the radio until you take it back.

When the Core runs short

Your call: the device with transmit keeps its full band and sound, and the others slow down first. The chip stays while it lasts, like the one on cellular.

Receivers and shared settings

Every receiver in use

A new slice that fits no receiver’s window, with none free, means taking one. It lists who has each receiver and what they’re doing, and you pick.

Your receiver taken

The other side: this phone’s slice closed, and it says who took the receiver and when. The connection stays up, and Take it back asks first.

Moving a shared receiver

Your call: you and the MacBook share a receiver, so going to 20 m asks first. With a receiver free, it would say slice B moves there instead of closing.

Shared settings

Your call: any device can change what every device hears, like the attenuator here, but it asks first when another device’s slices would feel it, and names them.

Told afterwards

The device that didn’t ask gets a note saying who changed what. Nothing to answer.

Who’s connected

Setup, Devices splits the devices connected now from the ones only paired, with each one’s slices and bands, and TX on the one with transmit.

More than four

A fifth device

Your call: a fifth device picks one of the four to take the place of. It starts on the one idle longest. Picking the one on the air turns the button red, and the Core unkeys it first.

Your own session

After a lost link, the phone takes its own session back without asking: its slices, and transmit if no one took it meanwhile. You’re never locked out of your transmitter.

On the device whose place was taken

The iPad is told who took its place and when. Its band stops, its slices and settings stay on the Core, and Take it back asks the fifth-device question the other way.

Transmit time-out

Your call: from a phone or iPad, the station ends any transmission that runs past the time-out, three minutes to start with, which is Thetis's own default. The PTT is a toggle, so a missed second tap or a headset button pressed in a pocket would otherwise keep the radio on the air while the link is working fine. The desktop at the station keeps Thetis's time-out switched off.

Setting it

On the PTT buttons page, as a station setting: from thirty seconds to thirty minutes, or off. Tap the value to step through a few.

When it fires

The station unkeys and the band says why. PTT is back to Tap, and one tap starts a fresh transmission.

New: long sessions

Hours of listening, on a stand or in a pocket. Locked, the phone asks the station for sound only, which saves most of the battery and data; the lock-screen card still gets its readings. iOS ends the card after eight hours, so the app leaves a useful last message on it.

Battery and sessions

Sound only while locked; your call: the screen always stays on while the band is showing; a sleep timer; and slowing the band in Low Power Mode or when the phone runs hot.

Back after a while locked

The band picks up where it is now, and the waterfall marks the stretch that was sound only, so nothing looks missing by mistake.

Eight hours in

Just before iOS ends the card, the app gives it a last message. The sound carries on; opening NereusSDR starts a fresh card.

A hardware PTT button

iOS won't let an app turn the microphone on while the phone is locked, except through Apple's own Push to Talk system. It recognises a button on a headset, and the Action button can run a "begin or end transmission" action. So a button can key a phone in your pocket, but only through Apple's system, and that bends your rule that a locked phone never transmits.

PTT buttons

The headset button, a paired Bluetooth PTT button and the Action button, all toggles like the PTT on the band. Whether they work with the phone locked is one switch.

Keyed from your pocket

Your call: a headset press keys the locked phone, through Apple's Push to Talk. The card turns red and Apple's blue pill and panel show. Another press unkeys; the card's UNKEY still waits for Face ID.

On the band

The PTT on screen follows the button, and says what keyed it.

Audio and data use

The station sends the band's sound as Opus at 24 kbit/s, the R3 plan's default, and the phone chooses how many frames a second of the band it asks for, which is most of the data. Data figures are estimates from the remote design's reference measurements until the bench measures ours.

Audio on this phone

Where the band plays and which microphone you talk into. The iPhone's own microphone keeps AirPods at full quality; theirs would drop both ways to phone-call quality.

From the band

Press and hold the speaker button to move the sound, without leaving the band.

Data use

One choice for Wi-Fi and one for cellular, with the cost of each per hour, a monthly counter and a warning.

On cellular

Your call: cellular starts at Balanced. The first time, the phone says so and what it costs; the chip stays while you're off Wi-Fi.

When things aren't right

Each screen names its own cause, so you know whether to walk to the radio, the station computer or your phone. Your call on versions: every app release still talks to a station one version behind, so an App Store update never locks you out; only a station two versions behind is refused.

The radio is off

The station answers but can't hear the radio. The desktop's DISCONNECTED over the band, with the last frame and the five-second retry.

The station isn't answering

The station computer is off, asleep or offline. The phone says what it tried and what to check.

The station needs updating

Only when the station is two versions behind this app. The phone names both versions instead of failing without a reason.

An older station

One version behind, or less: the phone connects and greys only what the station can't do yet.

This phone is offline

Not the station's fault. The phone waits for a network, and says the station has already unkeyed.

FreeDV Reporter

The desktop's FreeDV Reporter window, fitted to a phone: the same 14 columns folded into two lines per station, the same transmit, receive and message tints fading after six seconds, the band filter, QSY, lookups and your status message. The reporter connection runs at the station, so your station stays listed while the phone is away.

Stations on the air

Rust while a station transmits, slate while it's hearing someone, mauve when its message changes. Your call: a tap tunes there, as a double-click does on the desktop.

Press and hold a station

Every column for that station, with the desktop's right-click actions: tune, ask to QSY, look up, copy the callsign.

Ask to QSY

Spots on the band

The desktop's spot overlay, carried over as it is. Callsigns start halfway down the spectrum, stack down three levels and overflow into amber +N badges, coloured by DXCC: red a new country, orange a new band, gold a new mode, grey worked before. The spots come from the station, which runs the cluster, POTA and other spot clients; how they look is set on each phone. On the phone they never start behind a flag.

The station's side

The station is NereusSDR's daemon, running beside the radio. The remote design gives it three places to show its pairing code: on a computer, a Setup page; on a small box, a status page any browser on the network can open, and its console. Only the console or an already-paired device can reopen pairing.

On a computer: waiting

Your call: a switch in the desktop app, on a new Remote Station page under CAT & Network. The station keeps running after the app closes, so the phone can still reach the radio. Until a device pairs, the code shows here.

On a computer: adding a device

Once claimed, the page lists paired devices with Revoke, and Add a device shows a one-time code, the same one the phone's Devices page makes.

On a small box: its status page

Any browser on the network can open it. It only shows: nothing can be changed from it, so a neighbour on the Wi-Fi can't reopen pairing.

On a small box: its console

The code is in the log too, so a station out of reach can be claimed over SSH.

First launch

What someone sees the first time they open the app from the App Store, before they have a station. It follows the pairing design: a new station waits for its first device, one tap claims it on the same Wi-Fi, and its code works from anywhere.

Tuning dial

Your calls: all three, picked in Setup on this phone, on the page the desktop calls Navigation, and off until someone picks one. Each detent moves the active slice one step, with a haptic tick and a firmer bump on each whole kilohertz. The steps are Thetis's full list. Drag around a knob to try it. A browser can't make the ticks, so the mark at the top flashes instead.

Knob on the waterfall

Opposite PTT, so one thumb keys and the other tunes. Tap the middle to change the step.

Thumbwheel

Lock screen

Two iOS rules shape this: an app can't turn the microphone on while the phone is locked, and buttons on the lock screen wait for Face ID. Your calls: locking the phone unkeys, so a locked phone never transmits from its screen and the card only reports; the one exception is a hardware PTT button, through Apple's Push to Talk.
