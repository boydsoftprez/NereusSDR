## 1. Goal

The desktop 2026.10.1 moved to the workspace look: near-black surfaces in place of the navy, a raised gradient on buttons, one blue glass for a selection, lit green and amber that keep their meaning, and the muted red set with one stronger red for transmit. The phone still wears the navy of the 2026-09 design board. After this work the phone and the iPad look like the desktop they control, across every screen, with the same color for the same thing.

## 2. Non-goals

- No change to what any screen does, what it shows, or where a control sits, except Setup (section 7) and the iPad's Setup (decision W8).
- The spectrum and waterfall palettes, the band plan, the slice colors and the cyan frequency readout stay as they are (W2).
- No light theme and no theme switch. The phone keeps one dark palette, as today (`ios/NereusApp/Main/ChromeColours.swift:10`).
- No change to text sizes or fonts. Dynamic Type stays as it is; only Setup's layout at large sizes changes (W9).
- The desktop's own look does not change.
- Apple's own drawing stays Apple's: the status bar, sheets, the Live Activity card's outline and shape, and the Dynamic Island's black.

## 5. Treatments

A treatment is a token set applied to one kind of control. The phone gets one SwiftUI style for each, so a view asks for "raised" or "glass" and never repeats the values.

| Treatment | Where | What it draws | Desktop source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Panel | Setup sections, the RX and TX panels, sheets of our own | `kGlassPanelTop` to `kGlassPanelBot`, edge `kBorder`, radius 10 | `WorkspacePalette.h:348-350` |
| Raised | Every button at rest | `kGlassBtnTop` to `kGlassBtnBot`, edge `kBorder`, a 1 point light line on top | `WorkspaceComponents.cpp:1698` (`raisedFill`) |
| Glass | A picked choice, a Setup switch when on, Reconnect, a primary action | `kGlassSelTop` to `kGlassSelBot`, edge `kGlassSelBorder`, text `kGlassSelText` | `WorkspaceComponents.cpp:1010` (`glassTreatment`), `:1735` |
| Lit green | A running function | `raisedFillOf(shiftL(kGreenBg, +14))`, text `shiftL(kLiveGreen, +80)`, rim `shiftL(kDspToggleBorder, +16)`, top edge lifted 16 | `WorkspaceComponents.cpp:197-204`, `:243-256`, `:1292` |
| Lit amber | A measured or caution state, a muted phone | `raisedFillOf(kAmberBg)`, text `kAmberText`, rim `kAmberBorder` | `WorkspaceComponents.cpp:258`, `:1292` |
| Keyed | The keyed PTT, UNKEY | `kTxRed` with a lighter top, white text, rim `#e0a3a4` | `WorkspacePalette.h:324` |
| Danger | Cancel, Revoke, a refusal | `kBadgeTxBg` or `kRedBg`, text `kRedText`, rim `kRedBorder` | `WorkspacePalette.h:318`, `:325-327` |
| Inset | A number field, a meter's trough | `kInsetBg`, edge `kInsetBorder` | `WorkspacePalette.h:209`, `:214` |
| Disabled | Any control that can't be used now | the same control at 40 percent, in place, with its reason under it | the phone's existing rule |

Lit green and lit amber are computed by `shiftL` and `raisedFillOf`. Computed with Qt's own HSL arithmetic, as `shiftL` does it (`src/gui/styles/WorkspaceStyle.cpp:211`), and written into the phone's token file as literals with the formula beside them:

- Raised fill: top `shiftL(base, +16)`, `base` at 55 percent, bottom `shiftL(base, -12)` (`WorkspaceComponents.cpp:1710-1726`).
- Lit green: `#306247`, `#254d38` at 55 percent, `#1d3d2c`; text `#bbe1c6`; rim `#3e7e5c`, its top edge `#48946b`.
- Lit amber: `#4c3b16`, `#33280f` at 55 percent, `#20190a`; text `#d8a55f`; rim `#6b5426`, its top edge `#83672f`.
- Glass rim's top edge: `#4d89c1`.

The mockups used a sample of a desktop screenshot (`#2f6146` to `#1f412f`); the computed values replace it.

## 6. Where the colors live

Today the phone's colors live in role files: `ChromeColours` (`ios/NereusApp/Main/ChromeColours.swift`, 82 values), `BandColours`, `DialColours`, `ActivityColours` (`ios/Shared/Activity/ActivityColours.swift`), `SpotColours`, `FreeDVColours`, `ConnectChrome`, `ConfirmationSheetChrome`, and loose literals in 31 other view files (39 files under `ios/NereusApp` and `ios/Shared` carry a color literal).

**Design:**

1. **One token file, `ios/Shared/WorkspacePalette.swift`.** `ios/Shared` builds into both the app and the Live Activity extension (`ios/project.yml:55`, `:85`), so the widget stops repeating the app's values (today's reason for `ActivityColours`' own copies, `ActivityColours.swift:7-9`). It holds the tokens of section 4 and the computed values of section 5.
2. **The role files stay and point at the tokens.** `ChromeColours.panel` becomes `WorkspacePalette.panel`, and so on. Views keep reading the roles they read today, so the change in view code is small and each role's new value is reviewable in one table (appendix A).
3. **The treatments are SwiftUI styles in `ios/Shared/`:** a `ButtonStyle` for raised, glass, lit green, lit amber, keyed and danger; a `ToggleStyle` for the Setup switch; view modifiers for panel and inset.
4. **Loose literals in view files move to a role.** A literal that is a data color (a spot's mode, a FreeDV Reporter row tint, a slice color, a meter scale's zone) stays where it is and is listed in the plan as kept.
5. **The kept colors of W2 do not move** and get a test that pins them (section 10).

In `NereusKit`, only the band's ground changes. The desktop's workspace look draws the spectrum, the waterfall and the 3D view on `kAppBg` (`WorkspacePreview::dataBackground(..., "app-bg")`, `src/gui/SpectrumWidget.cpp:7301`, `:6910`, `:15652`) and keeps its frequency-scale strip `#101520` and dBm labels `#80a0b0` (`SpectrumWidget.cpp:7854`, `:8040`). So `BandRenderer.background` becomes `#08080a`, and the scale strip, the labels, the spectrum and waterfall palettes and the Display background color setting (`BandDisplaySettings.backgroundColour`, the desktop's `DisplayBackgroundColor`, whose default stays `#0A0A14` on both) do not change.

## 7. Setup

Setup is the one place the layout changes as well as the colors. Setup today is a `NavigationStack` with stock grouped `List`s on every page, the iPad included (`ios/NereusApp/Setup/SetupTab.swift`, `SetupTree.swift`). Its pages are built from one set of row kinds (`DescribedControl.swift`: switch, number, slider, menu, text field, color, button, reading, table, panel), so the change is made once in those rows and the page container.

### 7.1 Every page

- **Panels:** each section is a workspace panel (panel treatment) on the `kAppBg` page. Section names are in `kTitleText`, the color of the desktop Setup's group titles.
- **Kept:** the iPhone's back button and large page title, swipe back from the left edge, the Core / This phone / Both marks, the footer notes, and every word on every page.
- **Tags:** Core, This phone and Both become chips rounded at 7.
- **Choices:** the picked row is in the blue glass. The check mark stays, so the choice reads without color.
- **Switches (W7):** the iOS switch shape (capsule and knob) in the new colors: on is the glass, off is the inset. A switch that can't be used now stays in place at 40 percent, with its reason under it. VoiceOver still reads it as a switch, so it stays a SwiftUI `Toggle` with a custom style.
- **Numbers:** the value sits in an inset field with raised − and + buttons beside it, like the desktop's spin boxes. Tapping the value still opens the number pad at the foot of the tab, as today (`SetupNumberRow.swift`).
- **Menus:** a raised button showing the value and the up-down chevrons.
- **Sliders:** the track in the inset, the filled part in `kGlassSelBorder`, a light raised thumb.

### 7.2 Large type (W9)

At the accessibility text sizes, a number row puts the value field and its − and + under the label, and a menu row puts its button under the label. Switches stay at the right of their label. The trigger is SwiftUI's `dynamicTypeSize.isAccessibilitySize`, so the layout follows the user's own setting. Shown in setup-board.html, "Navigation, large type" and "AGC/ALC, large type".

### 7.3 The iPad (W8)

In a regular-width window the iPad shows the Setup list on the left and the open page on the right, as the desktop's Setup window does. The open category expands under its name in the list, and the open page is marked in the glass. A page's content is held to a readable width. In a compact-width window (Split View, Slide Over, or an iPhone) Setup is one list with pages that slide in, as today. The iPad's own tab bar stays (`ios/NereusApp/iPad/IPadLayout.swift`).

The router that opens a Setup page from elsewhere in the app (`SetupRouter.swift`) opens it in the right-hand side on the iPad and pushes it on the iPhone.

### 7.4 How the plan builds it

The pages can be built by restyling the stock `List` (hidden background, row backgrounds, custom section headers) or by replacing it with a scroll view of panels. The plan settles it with a one-page spike against setup-board.html, keeping whichever renders the mockup and also keeps VoiceOver order, Dynamic Type and swipe to delete (the rotor page's list uses it, `RotorSetupPage.swift:265`). That is an engineering choice, not a design one: the result looks the same either way.

## 11. Licence

The phone's licence is the GPL with an App Store permission that "covers only code whose copyright holders have granted it" (`ios/LICENSE:687`). The desktop's workspace tokens and treatments are cited to Longpath (OE5SOS, GPL-3; for example `WorkspacePalette.h:318-328`, `:348-369`, and the formulas in `WorkspaceComponents.cpp` marked "From Longpath src/gui/StyleConstants.h").

**Ruled by JJ, 2026-10-21: "use them and carry on".** The phone carries the desktop's Longpath-derived tokens and treatments. `ios/Shared/WorkspacePalette.swift` keeps each value's Longpath cite exactly as `WorkspacePalette.h` has it, so where every value came from stays visible, and `scripts/verify-ios-provenance.py` adds Longpath and OE5SOS to the upstream names it refuses, with one allowance: they may appear only in the two workspace files under `ios/Shared/` that carry the palette and its treatments, citing this ruling. A Longpath cite anywhere else in `ios/` still fails.
